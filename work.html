A story from my work

Making room to build.

Our Azure environment needed clearer structure and resource identification. Together with my cloud team, I helped reshape it around landing zones, shared Bicep templates, and GitHub deployment workflows.

My pieces were naming and tagging, networking, private DNS, and Azure Front Door. Then I partnered directly with developers as they began deploying applications and databases into the new environment.

What I worked on ↓

What I did

    Where I got hands-on

    Making it work for people.

    Making resources easier to understand

    I helped introduce naming and tagging conventions aligned with Microsoft’s Cloud Adoption Framework. The aim was practical: make it easier to understand what a resource is for.

    Connecting the pieces

    I configured virtual networks, VNet peering, private DNS zones, and DNS records. That networking is what let the services the team was bringing in reach each other.

    Sharing what I know

    I set up Azure Front Door and managed its endpoints. I also worked through Azure Firewall behavior with teammates, explaining how rule collections and rules support the access their services need.

    Helping developers get started

    I stayed involved as developers began using the environment, working with them on application deployments in the online zone and database setup in the corporate zone.

    What we built together: a more structured Azure environment, resources with clearer purpose, and a foundation developers had begun using. That connection between infrastructure and the people using it is what I want to keep building.
