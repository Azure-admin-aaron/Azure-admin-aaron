PERSONAL EXPERIMENT · RASPBERRY PI

A small server.
A lot to learn.

My Raspberry Pi has become a place to turn curiosity into something useful. Running Docker services and working through DNS and reverse proxying has taught me how much the pieces depend on one another.

See what I’ve been learning ↓

A QUESTION I KEPT COMING BACK TO

Where did the request go?

A service could be running on the Pi and still be hard to reach. Tracing the request—from a device on my network to the service it was looking for—helped me understand what DNS and the reverse proxy were actually doing. The map follows that path through my lab.

It starts with a name

A device on my home network asks for a local service by name. I learned to treat that name as the start of the trail, not the whole answer.

This follows a request through my setup, not separate machines. DNS, the reverse proxy, and the destination service can all run on the Pi.

WHAT STAYED WITH ME

The small discoveries add up.

01

A running service isn’t enough

A container can be healthy while the service still feels unavailable. Working through name resolution and routing taught me to ask where the request stopped.

02

Notes make the next try easier

Docker makes experiments easier to revisit. Writing down why I changed something helps when I return to it after the next idea has pulled me away.

03

There’s room to compare notes

The most useful outcome isn’t a perfect diagram. It’s having something concrete to share when someone else is working through a similar problem.
