PERSONAL EXPERIMENT · AI & AUTOMATION

Let agents explore.
Keep the controls clear.

Hermes is an AI-assisted paper-trading project running on my Raspberry Pi. It brings specialized research and analysis together with separate, rule-based checks before a simulated order can be placed.

Paper account only · No real-money trading

THE WORKFLOW

Different jobs, shared context.

Each stage passes evidence to the next. The roles have deliberately different permissions, so finding an idea is separate from approving and placing a paper order.

  1. 01

    Screener

    Finds possible candidates to investigate. A candidate is a starting point, not a trade.

  2. 02

    Hunter

    Gathers company and news context, then records the research features available.

  3. 03

    Mathlete

    Calculates technical indicators from completed daily market data.

  4. 04 · INDEPENDENT CHECK

    Brake

    Applies risk rules. It can veto a proposal or issue a short-lived, one-use approval.

  5. 05

    Sniper

    Checks the approval and paper-broker state again before submitting an allowed order.

  6. 06

    Reconciler

    Compares broker activity with local records and flags inconsistencies.

And a translator: Chief helps turn research and system output into a human-readable briefing. It does not approve risk or place orders.

WHY THE BOUNDARIES MATTER

Useful automation should be easy to question.

“AI agents” describes the collaboration, but not every decision is left to AI. Some stages collect or summarize information; the risk gate, approval checks, and audit trail are explicit controls.

One job at a time

The same component cannot both approve a proposal and execute it.

Missing data means pause

If important input is stale, incomplete, or conflicting, the system is designed to withhold approval.

A human can stop it

A HALT control blocks new activity. Clearing a halt is a human decision.

Leave a trail

Decisions are recorded so a result can be traced back through the checks that led to it.

STILL LEARNING

The data teaches as much as the agents.

Building this has made one lesson especially clear: a tidy workflow can still struggle when outside data is missing, delayed, or limited. The work now is to make those gaps visible, test assumptions, and understand why a proposal stopped instead of treating every rejection as a useful signal.

This is an ongoing experiment in orchestration and controls, not a claim about trading returns. The working code and account details remain private.
