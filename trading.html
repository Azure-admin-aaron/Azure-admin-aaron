PERSONAL EXPERIMENT · AI & AUTOMATION

Could agents help without taking over?

Hermes is my Raspberry Pi paper-trading experiment. I split research, analysis, risk approval, and order handling into separate roles so I can see where an idea moves forward—and where it stops.

Paper account only · No real-money trading

See how the project works ↓

HOW I SHAPED IT

One question, six narrower jobs.

I gave each part a smaller responsibility. A candidate moves through research and analysis, but only an independent risk check can allow a paper order. That split helps me see why a proposal moved forward—or why it stopped.

  1. 01

    Screener

    I began with a shortlist of ideas to investigate. Being on that list never meant a trade was ready.

  2. 02

    Hunter

    Company and news research added context—and exposed how much depended on the sources being available.

  3. 03

    Mathlete

    I added analysis from completed daily bars so an unfinished candle could not change the story mid-decision.

  4. 04 · INDEPENDENT CHECK

    Brake

    This was the line I wanted to hold: a separate risk check could say no, even after promising research.

  5. 05

    Sniper

    Even with approval, the paper order gets one more check against the broker before anything is submitted.

  6. 06

    Reconciler

    The last question is whether the paper broker and my own record agree about what happened.

04 / 06 · INDEPENDENT CHECK

Where I drew the line

Brake exists because a convincing research summary is not a risk approval. It checks the proposal and current state; missing data stops it.

Boundary: Brake may issue one short-lived approval, but it cannot place an order.

One more role: I added Chief to turn research and system output into a briefing I can read. It can help explain a decision, but it cannot approve risk or place an order.

A LESSON FROM BUILDING IT

The pause matters as much as the proposal.

A proposal can sound complete while an important input is missing. That experience pushed me to keep the risk check separate from the parts that gather evidence. The system needs to be able to say “not enough information” and stop.

Approval stays separate

I kept risk approval apart from execution, so a promising result cannot submit its own paper order.

Data gaps stay visible

A missing or conflicting input should show up as a problem to investigate, not masquerade as a thoughtful rejection.

The human stop remains

A HALT control blocks new activity. Clearing it stays a human decision.

Decisions leave a trail

The audit log helps me trace why a proposal moved forward or stopped.

STILL LEARNING

The surprising part was the data.

At one point, missing company-sector information caused proposals to stop at the risk gate. From the outside, it could have looked like the system was simply cautious. The real lesson was to check the inputs before reading meaning into the decisions.

I’m still working on making those gaps easier to spot and the whole system easier to review. It remains a paper-trading experiment, not a claim about returns. The working code and account details are private.
