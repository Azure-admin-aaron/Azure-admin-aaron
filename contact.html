A STORY FROM MY WORK

From unclear resources to a shared foundation.

One project captures how I like to work. Our Azure environment had limited documentation, and it was hard to tell what resources were for. I joined my cloud team in giving it clearer structure through landing zones, naming conventions, and tagging.

We built the Bicep templates and GitHub deployment workflows together. As the platform, management, online, and corporate zones took shape, I worked with developers to begin deploying applications into the online zone and databases into the corporate zone.

WHERE I GOT HANDS-ON

Here’s what I brought to the work.

Making resources easier to understand

I helped introduce naming and tagging conventions aligned with Microsoft’s Cloud Adoption Framework. The aim was practical: make it easier to understand what a resource is for.

Connecting the pieces

I configured virtual networks, VNet peering, private DNS zones, and DNS records. This was my part in connecting the services the wider team was bringing into the environment.

Sharing what I know

I helped set up Azure Front Door and manage its endpoints. I also worked through Azure Firewall behavior with teammates, explaining how rule collections and rules support the access their services need.

Helping developers get started

I stayed involved as developers began using the environment, working with them on application deployments in the online zone and database setup in the corporate zone.

What we built together: a more structured Azure environment, resources with clearer purpose, and a foundation developers had begun using. That connection between infrastructure and the people using it is what I want to keep building.
